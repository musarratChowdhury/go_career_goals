# 18 — How Hiring Managers Read GitHub (Help Wanted / Leonid Bugaev)
- URL: https://helpwanted.dev/blog/how-hiring-managers-read-github
- Accessed: 2026-10-26
- Published: 2026-09-01
- Source type: industry
- Credibility: 4 — Specific, behaviorally detailed account written by an experienced open-source insider (Leonid Bugaev, Grafana co-founder), describing reviewer click-paths rather than generic advice; prescriptive framing keeps it from being pure data, but concrete red-flag lists match other independent sources.
- Recency: 5
- Bias: 3 — The site is a platform for finding open-source projects to contribute to, so it has an agenda ("contribute to OSS, not toy repos"), though it repeatedly concedes most panels never open GitHub at all.

## Verbatim quotes
> "Employers do look at GitHub. Not all of them, and not as a ritual. When they do, they do not study the contribution graph. They open a handful of links and decide whether you can work in someone else's code." — intro
> "If nobody on the panel opens GitHub, none of this matters for that loop. If someone does, the green squares are the least useful thing on the page." — intro
> "\"Looks active\" is not a hiring signal. \"This person shipped a specified change and survived review\" is." — "They start at your profile, then they leave"
> "Size relative to the issue. A three-file fix for a named bug is readable. A 40-file \"cleanup\" attached to a one-line ticket is a red flag." — "The merged PR is the artifact"
> "An open PR that has sat for six months is unfinished work, not ambition." — "What they skip (and why you should stop optimizing it)"
> "It does not mean every recruiter clones your history. Many never leave the ATS. Some only check that the username on the application loads." — "What 'looks at GitHub' does not mean"
> "They may also open a PR you are not proud of. That is why spray-and-pray nits are expensive: the worst merge is still public. One or two repos with a few honest PRs is easier to defend than twenty typos." — "How they use it on a call"

## Relevance
- Bears on hypotheses: H3 refutation (confirms most recruiters never leave the ATS and some panels never open GitHub), H1 stress-test (when reviewers DO look, most portfolio surface — green squares, language bars, star counts, open-PR counts — is explicitly skipped or read as a red flag), and the general premise "projects impress CV reviewers" (impressiveness, when it exists, lives in merged-PR review threads, not in self-built projects).
- 3-line summary: A hiring-insider blog describes exactly how GitHub is actually read: not the contribution graph, not pinned repos, not stars — a handful of merged PRs with survivable code-review threads, judged against linked issues. Crucially for backfire risk, it lists concrete red flags reviewers punish: oversized diffs, open PRs sitting for months, typo-spray, and any public PR the candidate cannot narrate. Its implicit verdict: a typical solo portfolio repo (no external review, no issue thread, self-merged) is structurally not the artifact reviewers reward.
