# 04 — HackerRank 2025 Developer Skills Report (survey of 13,732 devs/EMs/recruiters)
- URL: https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025
- Accessed: 2026-10-26
- Published: 2025 (survey fielded "Q4 2024 and early 2025")
- Source type: industry
- Credibility: 4 — large multi-country survey (13,732 developers, engineering managers, recruiters, executives and students across 102 countries) plus platform data; primary data, though self-interested publisher
- Recency: 5 — 2025 report, current at time of research
- Bias: 3 — HackerRank sells hiring assessments, so framing consistently argues that assessments should matter more; underlying percentages are still real survey results

## Verbatim quotes
> "We also surveyed 13,732 developers, engineering managers, recruiters, executives, and students across 102 countries in Q4 2024 and early 2025." — section "Methodology"
> "Early-career developers have been hit the hardest, with junior hiring lagging behind as companies prioritize experienced talent." — Introduction
> "Year-over-year, hiring activity for: Lead developers is up 22% — Senior developers is up 19% — Junior developers is up just 9% — Entry-level hiring is nearly flat at 7%" — Insight #1, "Hiring recovery favors senior talent" chart bullets
> "Higher expectations – Companies are seeking hires who can deliver immediate value, rather than investing in junior training." — Insight #1, factors behind weak early-career hiring
> "Developers overwhelmingly prefer practical coding challenges over theoretical tests. 66% say they’d rather be evaluated on real-world skills, and 96% believe problem-solving should matter more than memorization." — Insight #5
> "Yet, most hiring tests don’t reflect the actual work. 78% of developers say assessments don’t align with real-world tasks, and 56% find algorithm-based questions irrelevant to their jobs." — Insight #5
> "Tech leaders recognize the gap. Ensuring assessments are relevant ranks as their top concern, even above bias prevention and assessment integrity." — Insight #5
> "If developers could fix one thing about hiring, it would be replacing algorithm-heavy tests with real-world projects that show how they solve problems in practice." — Conclusion

## Relevance
- Bears on hypotheses: H1 (real-world engineering work is what both sides want evaluated), H4 (entry vs senior demand asymmetry), partially H3 (no direct data on repo-clicking)
- Quantifies the level asymmetry for H4: hiring activity up 19-22% for senior/lead roles but only 7-9% for junior/entry — entry-level candidates face a structurally harder funnel, so project proof carries more of their burden; employers worry early-career hires "can't deliver immediate value."
- For H1, both developers and tech leaders say the same thing: evaluations should be real-world tasks (relevant assessments are tech leaders' #1 concern), i.e. a project that mirrors real production work (tests, deployment, trade-offs) signals more than algorithmic showmanship.
- Not evidence for H3: the report surveys attitudes toward assessments and employer brand, and does not measure whether reviewers open GitHub links — that gap must be filled by discussion sources (see 05).
