# 19 — Fortune/CodePath survey: side projects and portfolios are the top non-interview hiring signal (counter-case steel-man)

- Accessed: 2026-10-26
- Published: 2025-12-18
- Source type: industry
- Credibility: 4 — Exclusive reporting by a major outlet on a named primary survey (n>200 engineering leaders) from a known nonprofit (CodePath); main caveat is that it measures what leaders SAY matters, not what they actually click or hire on.
- Recency: 5
- Bias: 3 — CodePath is a training nonprofit whose mission is expanding early-career tech access, so a "portfolios matter most" finding aligns with its interests; mild, and Fortune's reporting is straightforward.

## Verbatim quotes
> "A new survey of more than 200 engineering leaders, conducted by tech training nonprofit CodePath and shared exclusively with Fortune, shows entry-level tech hiring is slowing. More than one-third of respondents, 38%, said their company has reduced the number of entry-level hiring over the past year, and nearly 1 in 7 reported pausing Gen Z hiring altogether." — main body
> "When asked which signals matter most outside the interview process, engineering leaders indicated proof of real-world skills matter far more than formal credentials. Side projects or portfolios topped the list, cited by 38% of respondents, followed by internship experience (35%), and public code portfolios like GitHub (34%)." — "The new secret to landing a tech job"
> "Traditional markers of achievement, by contrast, carried far less weight. Just 4% of leaders said credentialing programs were a top influence in hiring decisions, while only 23% cited a candidate degree or academic focus and 17% pointed to school prestige." — "The new secret to landing a tech job"
> "Greater fluency with AI tools and frameworks was the most common skill expectation for early-career hires, followed by faster time to writing production-ready code and the ability to learn new tools or programming languages quickly." — "The new secret to landing a tech job"
> "As entry-level tech hiring slows, new data shared exclusively with Fortune finds portfolios and internships are becoming the top readiness indicators for employers." — photo caption (Maskot—Getty Images)

## Relevance
- Bears on hypotheses: counter-evidence against H3 refutation and against the strongest "projects never impress" claims — the best recent survey data says side projects/portfolios are the #1 signal engineering leaders name outside the interview, ahead of degrees and school prestige; H1 stress-test in the other direction (portfolio emphasis is real but sits in a shrinking entry-level market where 38% of firms cut junior hiring).
- 3-line summary: CodePath's survey of 200+ engineering leaders (Dec 2025) finds side projects/portfolios (38%), internships (35%), and public GitHub portfolios (34%) are the most influential non-interview signals — degrees (23%), school prestige (17%), credentialing (4%) trail far behind. This is the steel-man for the premise that building projects impresses CV reviewers. Important qualifier: it captures stated influence of signals, not verification that reviewers actually open the repos, and it lands amid slowing entry-level hiring (38% of firms reduced it).
