# 16 — Why GitHub Won't Help You with Hiring and What Will (Underdog.io)
- URL: https://underdog.io/blog/why-github-wont-help-you-with-hiring
- Accessed: 2026-10-26
- Published: 2026-06-23
- Source type: industry
- Credibility: 3 — Well-argued and internally coherent, but written by a talent-marketplace vendor with a commercial motive to demote GitHub-first screening; several supporting statistics are secondhand claims it attributes to other pages rather than primary data.
- Recency: 5
- Bias: 2 — The article exists to sell Underdog.io's curated-marketplace alternative to GitHub sourcing; it explicitly argues "GitHub won't help you with hiring" while profiting from the replacement funnel.

## Verbatim quotes
> "The most common bad advice in technical hiring is simple: check GitHub first." — opening line
> "That's why GitHub won't help you with hiring, at least not in the way founders, recruiters, and startup hiring managers hope it will. It's a narrow signal, easy to overread, and badly matched to the reality of startup work." — intro section
> "Forked tutorials, old side projects, weekend experiments, abandoned repos, generated commits, and one-person toy apps all create visual activity. None of that tells you how someone handles a production incident, joins an existing architecture, or works through a vague product requirement with design and product." — "Volume isn't the same as evidence"
> "A hiring process built around GitHub tends to reward: Public hobby output over private professional impact / Free time over role relevance / Aesthetic repositories over messy production competence" — "Startup hiring needs a different lens"
> "Most recruiters don't have the time or technical depth to inspect repositories seriously. Most hiring managers don't have the bandwidth to manually review dozens of public profiles alongside resumes, sourcing, scheduling, and interviews." — "It doesn't scale as a hiring process"
> "The expensive part of hiring isn't the interview loop. It's believing you had a good signal when you didn't." — "The cost shows up before the hire"
> "If a signal systematically hides strong candidates and flatters weak ones, it shouldn't sit at the top of your funnel." — Practical rule callout

## Relevance
- Bears on hypotheses: H3 refutation (reviewers rarely inspect repos at scale), H1 stress-test (a portfolio-heavy CV strategy is built on a signal hiring teams say they cannot scale or trust), and the general premise "projects impress CV reviewers".
- 3-line summary: A talent-marketplace vendor argues GitHub is a weak, noisy, unscalable hiring signal that flatters visible hobby output over private production competence and systematically misreads both strong and weak candidates. It claims only ~15-20% of GitHub profiles contain substantive, well-documented code and that GitHub-first funnels produced mis-hires in ~40% of cases (both secondhand, unverified claims). For the investigation: credible-as-argument, not credible-as-data — but it steel-mans the "portfolio signal is overrated" position better than any hot-take thread.
